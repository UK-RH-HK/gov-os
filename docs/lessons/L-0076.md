During the bridge lifecycle, a need arose to account for completed roles that lacked formal checkpoint artefacts. The risk was that retroactive checkpoints could be fabricated and presented as if they had been produced contemporaneously, manufacturing a history that never existed.

The owner directed that checkpoints for already-completed roles lacking a formal record should be reconstructed only from durable evidence (run reports, commits, test outputs), labelled explicitly as `RETROSPECTIVE_CHECKPOINT_RECONSTRUCTION`, and should cite the underlying evidence they were derived from. No reconstructed checkpoint was to be presented as a contemporaneous worker checkpoint.

The lesson learned was that historical reconstructions are legitimate when needed for continuity or verification, but they lose their value — and become harmful — if they are not clearly distinguished from records produced at the time of the work. Labelling the provenance of a reconstruction is what separates honest gap-filling from manufactured history.

**Source:** OWNER-DIRECTION-BR-0004 (OD-BR-04) §2D, "Do not manufacture historical evidence."
