## Headline

1. **One lead verification runs the full `gov check` up to three times, not once.** The lead runs it itself (median 15.5 min wall, 24 min on 2 Nov; n=39). W2-54's "green here" case runs it again inside every scoped run (median 870 s; it is the scoped run's critical path). The serial phase runs it a third time even though it is routed to the nightly. Each run uses the real models twice (retrieval regression on the dev tiers, and the contexts of every ticket built twice). In 154 lead returns that report it, about 98 report it RED, and the RED is almost always not the ticket's doing.
2. **The leads' serial phase ignores the product's nightly route.** `gov close` leaves `nightly:` entries of `serial-only.txt` out (`gov.close.serial.phase`). The lead brief says to run "the list's entries". Since 31 Oct, lead serial phases spent about 5.0 h on nightly-routed cases (17 runs), and those cases account for most of the serial reds. This is how the real-model tests (W1-19 and W1-21: 212 to 852 s and 84 to 1,061 s; warm p95 failed 5 of 9 runs, at 0.59 to 9.1 s against 0.5 s), live paid-model sessions and the context-reproducibility `gov check` reach tickets' runs.
3. **The guard's ~9,000 tests are already mostly out of tickets' runs.** Since DEC-968 and DEC-1045, a lead's scoped run carries only `tests/unit/guard` (1,103 cases, 36 to 173 s CPU, about 1 to 2 %). The big suites come in only when (a) one of the 24 tickets whose paths hold `src/gov/guard/**` is changed, (b) a false HELPER reach applies (W1-47's 980 cases, 260 to 1,057 s CPU, pulled in because they import W1-07's support module), or (c) a batched close runs (13 to 17 % of its CPU). W2-50 (636 cases, up to 3,971 s) was gitleaks, not the guard, and is nightly-only now.
4. **The 1,619,286 tokens of `gov context` are a counting bug, not a real load.** `_read_record` reads the record's *file*, and every decision's file is the whole 1.66 MB register. So each cited DEC counts about 416k tokens, and all of them get the same sha256. The packet carries **no text** of its mandatory inputs, only ids and hashes. Nobody uses the count except W2-04's new 6,000-token ceiling. W2-04 fixes the count, but with its fix, 57 to 76 tickets are refused over the ceiling. That refusal is what drives the DEC-1027 H trimming and the lead's step 6a. That is ceremony.
5. **The lead brief is 54,112 characters of common text** (22,401 on 19 Oct; 49 commits in 14 days). The 56k-character launch brief is about 17k tokens, at 3.31 characters per token measured over 170 launches. It is carried in every one of about 96 API calls, about $0.48 per launch (estimate). The brief also mandates about 106 decisions (240k characters), the w2 prompt and the WBS, but the leads do not read them: the median lead reads 8k characters of the register and 6k of the prompt. The brief is grown by "brief gaps": 533 lines and 7,669 stated minutes in 83 returns. It holds stale lines (until W2-58 lands, until W2-70 merges; both are closed) and two self-contradictions (collect-only and `trailer_gate.py`, each "lifted" and still required).
