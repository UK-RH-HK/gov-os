# INVESTIGATION-1, part C: the CTO's own tools and practices (DEC-1047)

**Headline (5 lines)**
1. The CTO is the most expensive agent in the system: 337 to 450 M context tokens a day (10-30 to 11-02), an average of 415k to 526k tokens per turn, up to 890k before compaction. That is about $100 to $150 a day (*estimate*). About 90k of each turn is the standing prompt and the start-of-session reading.
2. 11 to 24 % of that spend goes to records, the register and pings. Another 12 to 31 % goes to watching from inside the session (inbox waits, tails, dispatcher reads). Each wake costs a whole ~430k-token turn. Only ~20 % of this week's 783 commits on `w2/integrate` touch `src/` or `tests/`; 608 are the orchestrator's (78 %).
3. The owner pays in interruptions. In the last two days: 131 permission prompts (122 of them `rm`, mostly in scratch, `/tmp` or `__pycache__`), 47 pings, 40 owner decisions, 25 packages on 11-02 alone, and 31 ACTs to date (10 exist only for the CTO's own safety nets).
4. The machine pays where the CTO's gates disagree. The load-based `gate_check.py` still gates the nightly while the Load Balancer measures real CPU (at 21:46 the load was 42.3 with 13.8 busy cores). Two of the last five nights gave no verdict because of preconditions a fresh clone does not need. `heavy_slot.sh` is not first-come first-served, and nothing records how long anyone waits.
5. Keep the dispatcher's core, the Load Balancer, the nightly, RESUME HERE and one stall net. Re-baseline the 115 KB prompt. Batch records and pings. Lift the duplicate gates, the watcher stack, the dead copies, the per-decision prompt versions and the same-day practice-to-product ratchet.

**heavy_slot.sh (C-11): why it is not FIFO.** Each waiter loops: `prio_waiting` → for i in 1..3 `flock -n` slot i → else `sleep 10` (priority callers `sleep 2`). There is no queue and no arrival order, so the first poller after a release wins, whatever its arrival. A newcomer that arrives just as a slot frees takes it at once. A non-priority waiter waits while any live priority waiter exists (`gov-os-heavy-prio-<pid>` files), with no limit. The nightly's `--held` takes all three locks itself (`nightly.sh:153-177`). Nothing writes a wait time: a grep of every orchestrator and worktree log finds 4 "waiting" lines in total. The dispatcher sees holders (`slot_holders` in `status.json`) but not waiters.
