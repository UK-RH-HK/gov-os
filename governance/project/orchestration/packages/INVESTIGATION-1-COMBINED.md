## 1. One page for the owner

**What the investigation found.** The protection the aim needs is small and mostly built: the five guard rules, independent test design and review, the dispatcher and the Load Balancer, the nightly, the memory tools, and the checkpoints that save work. The cost comes from five habits around it:
1. **The same check is run again and again, everywhere, in full.** Each lead verification runs the whole `gov check` up to three times. One test inside every scoped run starts yet another whole `gov check` (median 870 s). Leads run the cases the product sends to the nightly. Every red is re-run three times with the batch and three times without, against a clone built from the last nightly.
2. **Gates were built for problems an honest agent does not cause.** Examples: the Bash-reading parts of guard rules 1 and 5 (79 of 86 logged refusals were false), the strict close (0 of 30 direct runs passed), trailers and ratchet lists as refusals, and the context ceiling. That last one refuses packets that carry no text at all. Its 1.6 million tokens are a counting bug.
3. **The paperwork grows by itself.** The lead brief grew from 22k to 54k characters in 14 days, the CTO's prompt is 115 KB, and the register is 1.66 MB and grows about 55 KB a day. Every rule is copied into five places, every delegated choice becomes a register entry, and every CTO practice becomes a product KPI line the same day (W2-20 grew from 4 to 24 lines).
4. **The owner is interrupted for things that gate nothing.** On 1 and 2 Nov there were 131 permission prompts (122 of them `rm`, mostly in throw-away folders), 21 to 28 pings a day (24 of 86 said "gates nothing") and 21 to 25 packages a day. There were 8 ACTs on 2 Nov alone.
5. **The CTO watches from inside its own session.** It spends $100 to $150 a day (est.). 12 to 31 % of that context goes to waiting and tailing, and 11 to 24 % goes to records and pings.

**What the lifts would save** (at the recent rate, before the emergency; the items overlap, so these are orders of size, not sums to the minute):

**A fresh repository or this one? Keep this repository and lift here** (part E, confidence about 80 %):
1. The bureaucracy sits in configuration and practice, not in the repository's shape. Here a lift is one configuration line plus a decision (DEC-953, DEC-1045). A new repository would carry the same CTO practices along unless they are lifted anyway.
2. A copy cannot leave the gates behind. The memory stack imports 18,063 of the product's ~26,000 lines (77 modules), close and guard code included. A copy would also cut 18,360 DEC citations off their register, break W2-61's memory measure, and drop the evidence and the record branch.
3. Cost: fresh is about 8 to 14 working days, 3 to 6 owner-hours, and EXAMIN's copy 7 to 12 days later. Keep is about 2 to 4 working days (all est.). The middle way is to treat this list as the reset: keep what it keeps in one in-force file, and switch off or archive the rest.
