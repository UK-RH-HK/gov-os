# P-W2-51 — Two close checks read the owner's acceptance only from `Role: owner`: how they should read `Decided-by` (P1; gates only the orchestrator's self-commit of decision records)

**From:** the W2 orchestrator (W2-00, DAEO-sdxk), 2026-10-20 00:40 BST, as the owner's new standing rule asked (item 5) before the first commit of a decision record by the orchestrator.

## 1. Context
The owner's new standing rule (2026-10-20): the orchestrator commits the records of the owner's decisions itself, as soon as they are written (the register, the plan files, the prompt, the briefs, the path map and the ratchet lists), signed `Role: orchestrator`, `Task: decision-record`, `Decided-by: owner (chat, <date>)`, never `Role: owner`. The owner keeps the push, and the push is the owner's acceptance. Until now `Role: owner` on the commit has been the owner's approval fact: DEC-360 (decision files), DEC-724 (owner entries committed by the owner), DEC-757 and DEC-766 (the ratchet lists, owner commits only), and W2-12's `gov approve` is to make it a git fact (ADR-0001: the owner is the outside referee).

## 2. What happened (the code read on 2026-10-20)
| Where | Live? | What it asks | A `Role: orchestrator` commit of the owner's record |
|---|---|---|---|
| `src/gov/close/ratchet.py:104-111` (DEC-757, DEC-766) | **live, every close** | an `accepted_reds` or `load_sensitive` entry counts only if the commit that added it carries `Role: owner` alone | the entry "covers nothing and is a finding": the close refuses (fails closed) |
| `src/gov/close/state.py:172-200` (`_owners_entry`) | **live, when an escalation is lifted** | the register entry that lifts an escalation was brought in by commits "with the owner's role alone" | the escalation is not lifted (fails closed) |
| `src/gov/decisions/checker.py:342-366` (DEC-360) | dormant: no decision file exists yet, and `core-authority` skips `ACTIVE_UNAPPROVED` (`src/gov/check/authority.py:66`) | a decision file set ACTIVE by a commit with `Role: owner` alone | wakes with W2-03 (decisions as records) and W2-12 (`gov approve`) |

Not affected: the trailer gate (`gov.close.traceability`) judges only the commits of closed tickets (`Task: decision-record` is none), and it ignores an extra trailer; `core-authority`, `core-decision-citations` and the hooks (off) read no role of a register commit; the guard flags only a `Role: owner` commit made during an agent's call (`containment.py:1005`), which the orchestrator never makes. Every other register entry, the plan, the prompt, the briefs and the path map's namespaces are read by no owner check.

## 3. Why it matters
The two live checks guard the two places where an agent's mistake would open the close: a ratchet entry excuses a failing test, and an escalation lift releases a stopped ticket. Today `Role: owner` proves the owner put them there. If the orchestrator commits them, the checks must learn to recognise the owner's decision some other way, or they fail closed (safe, but the owner's decision would not take effect).

Ordinary work: yes, because the orchestrator will record ratchet entries and escalation answers in ordinary work. Consequence: other (fails closed: an owner's ratchet entry or lift has no effect; nothing fails open).

## 4. Options
- **(a) Keep `Role: owner` for these two kinds only; everything else per the new rule (recommended now).** The orchestrator commits every other decision record itself. The **ratchet entries** (`accepted_reds`, `load_sensitive`) and **an entry that lifts an escalation** stay owner commits: the orchestrator prepares them as a ready-to-commit patch with exact operator commands and pings `ACT`. No code change. Cost: a rare owner commit (ratchet entries come only from owner packages, DEC-766; there is no open escalation). Nothing else waits on the owner.
- **(b) The checks read the owner's acceptance from the push.** A ratchet entry or a lifting entry counts when its commit carries `Task: decision-record` and `Decided-by: owner (…)`, **and** it is an ancestor of the pushed ref (`refs/remotes/origin/w2/integrate` as the owner's push left it). The owner's push is then the acceptance in git, as the owner said. A judge change (CAP-33) in `src/gov/close/ratchet.py` and `state.py`, through CIT-P, about 60 lines with tests; it fits W2-12 (owner approvals as git facts), or a small ticket now. Risk: low, and it fails closed if the ref is stale (an unpushed entry does not count yet).
- **(c) The checks read `Decided-by:` alone.** No push needed. **Fails open:** any session can write that trailer by mistake, and a ratchet entry excuses a red test. Not recommended.

## 5. Recommendation
**(a) now, and (b) carried by W2-12 (with DEC-360's decision files), confidence medium-high.** (a) costs the owner almost nothing today; (b) makes the push the acceptance in git, as the rule intends, without a fail-open path. (c) is the fail-open option under the judgement rules.

## 6. What it blocks, and what waiting costs
It gates only the orchestrator's first commit of decision records under the new rule. Until the owner answers, the drafted records (DEC-861 and its places) wait in the main tree, **unstaged**, so no merge, close or lead waits on them. The leads (W2-62, W2-59, W2-50) keep running.
