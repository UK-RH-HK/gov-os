## Findings

**F1: cutting an expanded parent can drop the very line that was hit.** (S1; out of the KPI lines, which require
this; the question is DEC-1086 B's intent.) S1 cuts every item over 4,000 characters, and the brief's check list
confirms that expanded pieces are cut the same way; the test designer read S1 the same way (W2-135 README, N-3).
But an expansion replaces the hit child with its parent. When the
child lies past character 4,000 of the parent, the bundle sends neither the child's text nor the parent's tail. The
probe shows it: the hit word in step 65 of a 5,719-character section is not in the sent text. DEC-1086 B approved the
cap "as a guard against one-line files only". This cut reaches every multi-line section or Python module over 4,000
characters that the budget lets expand. The measure cannot see it, because `hit_at_5` and `in_bundle` judge by lines.
Options for the CTO, not a round: (i) keep, as the ticket says; (ii) when a piece is cut, send the 4,000 characters
around the hit child's lines rather than the first 4,000 (S1 re-worded); (iii) do not expand a parent whose pieces
would be cut (S3's "expansions unchanged" re-worded). My recommendation: ask the owner in one line whether the
cut should reach expanded sections. If not, (ii) is the smaller change. Moderate confidence.
- Ordinary work: yes, because sections and modules over 4,000 characters expand whenever the budget allows (the
  package's largest call without long lines is 4,633 tokens, so expansions of several thousand characters happen).
- Consequence: other (the item still cites the whole span and carries `cut`, so an agent can read the file; nothing
  fails open and no work is lost, but the bundle's best evidence can be missing from what is sent).

**F2: a narrowed register item keeps the parent's stale `cut` key, and is never cut itself.** (S2 and failure-1, in
scope; `src/gov/decisions/register.py`, outside the ticket's `allowed_paths`.) `register.leave_out` runs after
`retrieve` in `gov retrieve` (`retrieve/command.py:111`). `narrowed()` copies every key but the marks
(`{key: value for key, value in item.items() if key not in MARK + ("also",)}`). It then sets `text` to the chunk's
full decoded body. So a cut parent narrowed to a short chunk carries `cut: {"chars": <the parent's length>, "sent":
4000}` on whole text: the probe gives lines 20-32, 1,118 characters, `cut: {"chars": 5303, ...}`. A narrowed chunk
over 4,000 characters would also be sent whole. The fix that can ride along: in `narrowed()`, drop `cut` and pass the
text through the same cap (for example, a shared helper). That needs `register.py` in a ticket's paths.
- Ordinary work: no, because narrowing needs one parent spanning two register entries. That arises only with a
  non-Markdown register: a Markdown register is cut at every heading (W2-133's unit-test docstring, W2-131's
  README N-3), and this repository's register is `docs/DECISION_REGISTER.md`. W2-133's tests plant a `.txt` register
  to reach it.
- Consequence: other (a false mark on text sent whole; nothing fails open and nothing is lost).

**F3: merging turns 13 of W2-133's acceptance tests red on integrate.** (Out of scope: another ticket's acceptance
oracle.) `tests/acceptance/W2-133/test_w2_133_unchanged.py` compares today's bundle with `retrieve.py` and
`register.py` pinned at `a4e713517cac`, keeping every key but the register labels, `text` included. On the branch,
11 of its 26 parametrised S3 cases and both continued-page cases fail. Through `retrieve` alone, the failures are the
intended cut (8 items cut). Through `gov retrieve`, they are F2's stale key only (no text differs). With `text` and
`cut` taken off, every case is equal. The 48 pass on integrate. The CTO's scoped run (`tests/unit/retrieval` with
`tests/acceptance/W2-32`) did not include this suite. Recommendation: before the merge, or with it, an independent
test-designer pass on W2-133's oracle (take off `text` and `cut` for items over 4,000, as W2-135's own S3 does), or the
reds recorded as known when the merge lands. The engineer cannot touch `tests/acceptance/**`.
- Ordinary work: yes, because every run of `tests/acceptance/W2-133` after the merge (the nightly, the merge queue's
  checks, any scoped run that selects it) hits it.
- Consequence: other (fails closed: red tests on integrate; nothing opens and nothing is lost).

**F4: some of the measure's figures can move, contrary to the package's "the measures do not move".** (Out of scope:
`src/gov/retrieval/measure.py`.) `hit_at_5` and `in_bundle` judge by lines, and are unchanged. But `_ask` sizes each
stacked item from its sent text (`size = -(-len(item[3]) // TOKEN_CHARS)` against `--stack-budget 24000`). With
long items now ≤1,000 tokens, more continuation items can be stacked, so `full_evidence`, `pieces_found`, `calls` and
`stacked_digest` can change. `_match`'s family rule (W2-134 S5) looks for the gold text in the item's sent text, so a
family hit whose gold span lies past character 4,000 of a cut item is lost. Kept outputs from before the merge carry
whole text, so `--kept` re-scores them as before. Recommendation: the CTO names this when re-pin window 4's figures
are compared with the pin (DEC-1086 A's side-by-side check), and does not read a `full_evidence` or family-hit
difference as a regression of W2-32.
- Ordinary work: yes, because any measurement run after the merge on a set with long items, or with families
  declared, reads the cut text.
- Consequence: other (a measurement shift; nothing fails open and nothing is lost).

### What I checked, by KPI line

**S1: pass.** `capped(body, first)` returns the decoded text unchanged when it is ≤4,000 characters in all, or when
no line is over the cap. Otherwise it walks `body.splitlines(keepends=True)`, the same splitting `span()` and
`validate` use, so `lines` are the file's line numbers. It measures each line without its trailing `\r`/`\n`, sends
a line over 4,000 as its first 4,000 plus its own break, and sends every other line whole. Lines are split only at
`\n`/`\r` bytes, which never fall inside a multi-byte UTF-8 sequence, so decoding line by line gives the same
characters as decoding the whole. `cut` is `{"chars": <full text>, "sent": <sent text>, "lines": [...]}`. `item()`
sets `id`, `sha256` (of `body`), `path`, the lines, `chunk_id`, `routes` and `batch` from the full span as before.
**An ordinary long section is never cut:** with no line over the cap, `capped` returns the whole text and `{}`
(probe, 11,499 characters; the designer's 11,000-character logbook section; the unit cases). **F1 is resolved.**

**S2: pass.** No line over the cap means no key, whatever the total length (probe; acceptance success-2, 11 cases).
`register.narrowed()` no longer copies the parent's `cut`. It passes its own chunk's bytes through the same
`capped(body, chunk["start_line"])`, and adds a `cut` only where its own lines hold a cut line, placed after `text`.
The unit cases cover both: narrowed to ordinary lines gives no key and whole text; narrowed to its 4,500-character
line gives its own key, equal to `retrieve`'s for the same chunk sent alone. Acceptance (N-6) checks a `.txt`
register, as W2-133's does. **F2 is resolved.** The supplementary block also calls `leave_out`, with items that have
no `text`, so a narrowed item there gets a capped `text` and no key. But `supplementary.stack` re-reads each chunk's
text itself (`_text`) and never sends that field, so nothing it sends changes.

**S3: pass.** `used` is still counted from the bodies before `item()` (unchanged lines 543 and 610-613). The
reranker reads `_rerank_text` from the candidates. `item()` runs after every choice. The pool, the continuation,
`repeats`, `gaps` and `batches` read lines and ids only. The unit S3 case and acceptance success-3 (39) compare the
bundles field by field, the cut text and key apart, against `retrieve.py` and `register.py` pinned at
`e3355def9f99`, with the reranker's input recorded. With only long lines cut, W2-133's pinned comparison no longer
sees any difference (its planted lines are all short).

**failure-1: pass, as read with the re-worded S1** (R2-F5). No line over 4,000 is sent whole (the slice and the
`lines` entry come from one condition per line). No cut item lacks its key (the key is returned whenever `lines` is
non-empty, and both callers spread it into the item). In `narrowed()`, the key is added when the loop meets
`text`. Every bundle item from `item()` has `text`, so a cut narrowed item in `gov retrieve` always carries it.

**failure-2: pass.** Only `text` and the added key differ (S3's cases, my probes).
